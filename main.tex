\title{A resource theory of structuration: \\ Role differentiation as ignition of a collective information engine}

\author{Maximilian Puelma Touzel}
\email{puelmatm@mila.quebec}
\affiliation{Mila--Quebec AI Institute, 6666 St.\ Urbain Ave., Montreal, Quebec, Canada}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Informational active matter research has shown how measurement-informed decisions produce collective order, so far in systems that reach consensus. We identify a collective information engine that orders by differentiation instead, and construct a minimal instance using anti-coordination games, the simplest game class in which differentiated role information has value. Agents infer their role from a noisy social signal grounded in a persistent identity, and role-following action feeds back into that signal, which shapes the incentive to follow roles. Resources accrued through coordinated role-play combine with population variability to reinforce the schemas that generated them. The model thereby operationalizes Sewell's duality of schemas and resources, the qualitative resolution of sociology's structure--agency debate. The engine ignites when a loop gain $\Lambda$---the product of identity persistence, cognitive capacity, and social feedback channel fidelity---exceeds one. For a repertoire of such schemas, roles emerge with increasing gain in a bifurcation cascade whose functional form is fixed by the repertoire's eigenvalue spectrum, ranging from monitorable logarithmic sequences to spike-then-avalanche onsets that arrive without warning. Resource accumulation turns schema competition into a replicator dynamics that, in mean field, selects the cascade type endogenously. Subcritical behavioral covariance reveals that type before onset, which enables early detection, and feedback channel parameters bias which type is selected. For agents interacting on a platform, adaptive platform design then becomes a control lever to throttle emergent coordination, for example to mitigate risks from distributional AI. Joining game theory, collective dynamics, and information engines, we open a route to an information thermodynamics of agent populations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A century-old question runs through the social sciences: how does macro-level social structure shape individuals while, simultaneously, individuals' choices generate that structure? Giddens proposed \textit{structuration} as a resolution to this \textit{structure--agency debate} based on the \textit{duality of structure} \cite{giddens1984}: social structure is both the medium and the outcome of the schemas and resources it organizes. Soon after, another sociologist, Sewell, noted that Giddens's duality accounted only for the reproduction of schemas---essentially a local stability condition rather than an endogenous dynamics. Motivated to provide an account of schema transformation, Sewell recast the duality as a (qualitative) feedback process between \emph{schemas}, the transposable rules and conventions that give meaning to resources by prescribing behavior around their use, and \emph{resources}, the material and symbolic stocks that schemas mobilize and that empower the agents using them \cite{sewell1992}.
Each sustains the other: schemas direct the use of resources, and resource-wielding agents reproduce (or transform) schemas. Debates resonant with the same micro--macro tension, many unresolved, recur across social science (\textit{e.g.} in cultural evolution \cite{acerbi2015} and in macroeconomics \cite{lucas1976,solow2010}), An explicit dynamics of structuration would therefore bear on more than sociology.

Despite a century of agent modeling, Sewell's resource-schema structuration has no quantitative formalization. Surprisingly, existing agent modeling frameworks do not even operationalize its core objects. Opinion dynamics is studied using Ising-like models that do not represent resources. Cultural evolution is studied using replicator-type models (\textit{e.g.} evolutionary game theory, including spatial reaction-diffusion extensions) that derive from a single fixed, imposed game structure without resource accumulation. More specialized models treat resource accumulation \cite{bouchaud2000} and payoff-dependent network reshaping \cite{santos2006}, but represent schemas as scalar state or payoff terms rather than as semantically loaded, transposable rules. None treats the resource-schema dynamics central to Sewell's vision. Large language models have opened a new class of applications for agent modeling, both of human social systems \cite{vezhnevets2023} and of deployed AI agent systems \cite{anthropic2026}. Responses to the calls for applications from physics \cite{han2026,miehling2025} have so far been grounded in these existing approaches (\textit{e.g.} statistical mechanics of opinion dynamics \cite{el2026}, replicator-like mean field theories \cite{flint2026}), which lack schema-resource structuration. It is thus timely for physics to supply a quantitative theory of the latter.

We provide such a theory, exemplified for the case of role-prescribing social schemas, and we find that its natural language is thermodynamic. First demonstrated by Szilard almost 100 years ago \cite{szilard1929}, information engines---systems that convert measurement into extractable work through feedback, subject to performance limits set by measurement quality \cite{Castro2026}---quantify the value of information for physical agents. We show that a population of agents incentivized and provided with the means to role-play (via memory in an anti-coordination game) constitutes a \emph{collective, self-fueling information engine}: coordinated role-play is the payoff-generating output---the engine's analog of extracted work---and the role information measured bounds it, as Szilard's bound limits his illustrative model engine. As an instance of the long-noted link between Maxwell's demon and symmetry-breaking order parameters \cite{parrondo1999}, this description belongs to informational active matter research: a modern expansion of Szilard's ideas to more complex settings, where measurement-informed decisions produce collective order bounded by the information acquired \cite{vansaders2023, ziepke2022}. Those engines reach \emph{consensus}, a magnetization-like order. The engine here produces \emph{differentiation}, a covariance-like order in which agents take complementary rather than matching roles. Social role structure is the engine's running state, sustained by the burning of role information generated by that structure.

We formalize the feedback itself: for a given repertoire of schemas, which become institutionalized, in what order, and which agents are enriched are endogenous outcomes, while what a schema is \emph{about} is held fixed. Transformation in the strict sense requires in addition a mechanism holding schemas on distinct axes, which the game does not fix. The available choices differ in their assumptions and yield a family of theories (\cref{app:directions}). In this paper, we center the structuration that every such theory contains by scoping down onto a dynamics of the schema strengths for a fixed repertoire, in which the schema--resource loop already closes.

Our main results are: (i) a minimal model that couples schemas and resources through agent identities and actions, (ii) an ignition condition---$\Lambda>1$, where dimensionless loop gain $\Lambda$ factorizes into identity persistence, agent cognitive capacity, and social feedback channel fidelity---for self-sustaining role differentiation; (iii) an extension to multiple schema contexts results in a bifurcation cascade in which role dimensions activate sequentially, with functional form determined entirely by the eigenvalue spectrum of the context repertoire; (iv) an endogenous theory of that spectrum: resources convert schema competition into a replicator dynamics that, in the near-threshold mean field, selects among cascade classes, from single avalanches to logarithmic cascades; and (v) a monitoring protocol---subcritical spectral inversion of the behavioral covariance---that reads out the future cascade of the current repertoire before its onset, subject to finite-sample resolution and slow schema drift. We close by applying the monitoring framework to mitigate the risks associated with distributional AGI \cite{tomasev2026}: the hypothesis that general-level capability may emerge from a coordination phase transition among many individually limited AI agents.

\paragraph{The game.} We design utility into roles minimally, using a generic anti-coordination game. Two players choose Go or Defer: both playing Go is costly to both ($-1,-1$), both deferring is mediocre ($3,3$), and complementary role-play pays best on average, though asymmetrically ($6,2$). The latter is played in a role signal-based correlated equilibrium that pays $4$ per player, while the mixed Nash equilibrium pays only $2.5$.
Even though role-following is not a dominant strategy---it is obeyed only when partners also follow---its incentive constraint is strict once roles differentiate and agents seek to maximize their payoffs (\smref{S1\,C}).
The maximal coordination gain $w_0 = 1.5$ per encounter is the work available to an engine that assigns complementary roles through the measurement of social signals. The specific numbers are inessential: any anti-coordination game enters the theory only through this single scalar $w_0$, which in turn sets merely the scale of wealth.
What is essential is the anti-coordination \emph{class}, in which roles are incentive-compatible: each action must be the best reply to its opposite---given Defer, Go must pay more ($6>3$), and given Go, Defer must pay more ($2>-1$). Even equal off-diagonal payoffs would suffice. We set them unequal to keep the general case.

\paragraph{Agents, game contexts, and identities.} $N$ agents play the game against each other in pairwise encounters across a repertoire of $M$ distinct game contexts. We emphasize the importance of variable context in capturing both the transposability of schemas and the context-dependent nature of roles. We represent identities, $\w_i$ for agent $i$, and contexts, $\g_m$ for context $m$, as vectors $\w_i,\g_m\in\mathbb{R}^d$ with $d\gg1$ some embedding dimension. Each agent stores and presents its own identity in each encounter, though a public registry is an equivalent alternative implementation (\cref{sm:gain}). For simplicity, the identity of agent $i$ in context $m$ is contextualized as the scalar $\w_i\cdot\g_m$. When paired with $j$ in an encounter, agent $i$ makes a noisy observation of the relative value $s=\Delta_{im}(j)+\sobs\xi$, with signed status difference $\Delta_{im}(j)=(\w_i-\w_j)\cdot\g_m$---its sign selects the role, its magnitude sets how reliably that role can be inferred---and $\xi$ a standard Gaussian noise source. Averaged over the drawn partner $j$, this reduces to $\Delta_{im}$ below. The policy of the correlated equilibrium prescribes Go ($a=+1$; payoff 6) when $s$ is positive and Defer ($a=-1$; payoff 2) when negative.
%However, to keep the general case in mind, we use these asymmetric payoffs and
To reflect the unequal payoffs, we call $s$ relative \textit{status} (as in `social status'), though we emphasize that status is relative to the game context.
Integrating $T$ noisy samples, the Bayes-optimal decision is deterministic: $a=\sign(\tfrac1T\sum_{t}s_t)$.

Roles require persistent identities. We take a simple choice: each identity low-pass filters its agent's action history, evolving under a restoring force, a context-dependent signed action input, and write noise,
\begin{equation}
\dot{\w}_i = -\gamma\,\w_i(1+|\w_i|^2) + b_0\!\sum_{e\in E_i} a_e\,\g_{m_e}\,\delta(t-t_e) + \sdyn\bm{\eta}_i ,
\label{eq:agent}
\end{equation}
where $E_i$ is the set of encounters involving agent $i$, $a_e$ and $m_e$ the decision and context of encounter $e$, $b_0$ the per-encounter kick, $\gamma$ the decay rate of identity, and $\bm{\eta}_i$ a vector of standard Gaussian noise sources. This intrinsic write noise captures the effects of unobserved degrees of freedom beyond the encounters. \Cref{eq:agent} is an overdamped Langevin equation in a quartic confining potential: the restoring force is $-\nabla_{\w_i}\gamma(|\w_i|^2/2+|\w_i|^4/4)$, whose cubic term serves only to bound identity growth (\cref{sm:micro}). Its coefficient is tied to $\gamma$ as a choice of units for $|\w_i|$, absorbed by rescaling $\g_m$ and $\beta$, so the linear decay rate is the only free parameter of the restoring force. Encounters are Poisson at rate $\nu$, drawn across contexts with normalized frequencies $\sum_m f_m=1$ and over partners with pairing probabilities $p^{(m)}_{ij}$. Averaging each decision over observation noise (giving the expected signed action $\langle a\rangle_m$), over the drawn context and partner, taking the mean field ($N\to\infty$), and coarse-graining the Poisson kick stream in its dense-encounter diffusion limit ($\nu\to\infty$, $b_0\to0$ at fixed $\beta\equiv b_0\nu$), the drift becomes
\begin{equation}
\dot{\w}_i = -\gamma\,\w_i(1+|\w_i|^2) + \beta\sum_m f_m\,\langle a\rangle_m\,\g_m + \sdyn\bm{\eta}_i ,
\label{eq:agent-mf}
\end{equation}
where $\langle a\rangle_m$ is the expected signed action in context $m$---a sigmoid of the deterministic status difference, $\Delta_{im}$. Writing $\pi(\Delta_{im})=\Pr(a{=}{+}1|\langle s\rangle_{\xi}=\Delta_{im})$ for the noise-averaged Go-probability, we have $\langle a\rangle_m=2\cdot\pi(\Delta_{im})-1$, so its slope at zero difference is $2\pi'(0)$---the read gain that sets how strongly actions are written into memory (Bayesian policy on Gaussian noise gives $\pi'(0)=\sqrt{T/2\pi}/\sobs$).
For simplicity, we consider well-mixed pairing, which averages the partner projection to the population mean. This vanishes in the symmetric state, the undifferentiated population before any role structure exists, so the difference and thus $\langle a\rangle_m$ depend on $\w_i$ alone. For structured pairings, the effect enters through $p^{(m)}_{ij}$ as a graph Laplacian (see \cref{sm:micro} for extensions).

%Each context takes in an agent and returns a role prescription: $\g_m$ designates the status axis that carries role information and prescribes complementary play conditional on the sign of the difference along it. Role differentiation is then the direct consequence of schema-following: the decisions that $\g_m$ prescribes are inscribed back into identity along $\g_m$ (the $\beta$ term of \cref{eq:agent-mf}), so a population that follows a schema separates along exactly the axis the schema designates, exactly the duality making $g_m$ a schema in Sewell's sense.

\paragraph{Resources and schemas.}
Coordination income accumulates as agent wealth,
\begin{equation}
\dot r_i = \sum_m f_m W_{im} - c\,r_i, \;\;\textrm{with} \;\;W_{im}=w_0(1-2P_{e,im})^2
\label{eq:resource}
\end{equation}
the payoff above Nash at role-inference error rate $P_{e,im}$ (\smeq{S2}) and $c$ the resource-decay rate, so $1/c$ is the horizon over which past income still counts. This payoff obeys a Szilard bound, $W\le 2\ln2\,w_0\,I$ with $I=1-H_{\mathrm{bin}}(P_e)$ the role information a measurement supplies (\smref{S1}). This is the differentiation case counterpart of the information bound on consensus order case in active matter \cite{vansaders2023}. The bound is information-theoretic rather than a physical energy budget: $W$ is the coordination payoff above Nash, not thermodynamic work, and the model carries no heat, entropy production, writing cost, or schema-maintenance cost. The bound therefore fixes the engine \emph{analogy} without entering the ignition or cascade results. The symmetric form $I=1-H_{\mathrm{bin}}(P_e)$ is the equiprobable two-role idealization. Heterogeneous status differences and pairwise error rates enter through the partner average $W_{im}$ that follows. Here $W_{im}$ is a partner-average of the pairwise payoff $W_{ijm}=w_0(1-2P_{e,ijm})^2$, set by the magnitude of the status difference to the partner and averaged over the same pairing statistics $p^{(m)}_{ij}$ that enter \cref{eq:agent} (extensions treated in \smref{S2}).

Agents who profit from taking differentiated roles along a schema reinforce it. A schema is a vector, on whose two parts reinforcement acts separately: its direction $\hat\g_m$ fixes what the schema is \emph{about}, its magnitude how strongly that prescription is \emph{institutionalized}. Writing $\g_m=\sqrt{u_m}\,\hat\g_m$ with $u_m=|\g_m|^2$ splits any such dynamics exactly into a \emph{strength sector} carrying $u_m$---the reinforcement of a schema already in the repertoire---and a \emph{transformation sector} rotating $\hat\g_m$, the identity direction along which game $m$ anti-correlates the paired agents' actions.

We develop the strength sector, which suffices for schema-resource duality: it determines which of an available repertoire become institutionalized, in what order, and which agents are enriched. The $\{\hat\g_m\}$ are then fixed as environmental: the repertoire set of games available to be played. The dynamics of the transformation sector is set out for completeness in \Cref{app:directions}, wherein we show repertoire diversity depends strongly on what additional assumptions are made about how schemas interact.

The strength $u_m$ acts at each stage of an encounter. It scales the status difference read, $\Delta_{im}\propto\sqrt{u_m}$, which lowers the role-inference error rate $P_{e,im}$ against fixed noise $\sobs$. It also fixes the social gain $2\pi'(0)u_m$ and hence the coupling at which context $m$ bifurcates (\cref{sm:gain}), so the ordering of the $u_m$ is the order in which contexts activate across the cascade. The strengths also draw on one bounded collective capacity to signal and read status, $\sum_m u_m\le S_{\mathrm{bw}}$ (\cref{sm:closedpool}), so once the total reaches it, raising one lowers the rest. Institutionalization is thus the allocation of that capacity across the available games, and the dynamics below the rule by which coordination success reallocates it.

Each strength then grows in proportion to how much resource-weighted structure lies along its own axis, and decays at a rate common to every schema:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\dot u_m = 2u_m\bigl(\alpha\lambda_m-\kappa\bigr), \qquad
\lambda_m = \hat\g_m^{\!\top}\Cr\,\hat\g_m , \\[2pt]
\Cr = \frac{\sum_i r_i\, \w_i \w_i^{\!\top}}{\sum_i r_i}
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:schema}
\end{equation}
where $\Cr$ is the resource-weighted population covariance, $\lambda_m$ the resource-weighted variance along context $m$, $\kappa$ the context decay rate, and $\alpha$ the context reinforcement rate. Equivalently $d\ln u_m/dt=2(\alpha\lambda_m-\kappa)$, the factor of two because $u_m=|\g_m|^2$ grows at twice the rate of $\g_m$. The two ingredients of $\lambda_m$ encode Sewell's duality. It is \emph{covariance}-driven because a schema can organize only the behavioral variation that exists: only the differentiation already present along axis $m$ can be divided into complementary roles, so a schema is reinforced along the axes of differentiation the roles themselves produced. A schema aligned with no variance prescribes nothing and decays at rate $\kappa$. It is \emph{resource}-weighted because a schema is validated by the payoff its adherents accrue rather than by raw differentiation: weighting by wealth $r_i$ makes reinforcement contingent on success---the operational form of Sewell's claim that resources, accumulated by enacting a schema, sustain the schema that produced them. With reinforcement proportional to $u_m$, \cref{eq:schema} selects among existing schemas rather than creating new ones, at a per-unit rate set by $\lambda_m$. Because the decay is common to all schemas, the shares $u_m/\sum_n u_n$ obey a replicator equation with fitness $\alpha\lambda_m$ exactly. The finite bandwidth regulates the total (\cref{sm:closedpool}).

Of the three repertoire properties that fix the cascade (\cref{sm:cascade}), the strength hierarchy $\{u_m\}$ is therefore endogenous, while the coherence $\{\hat\g_m\cdot\hat\g_n\}$ and the aspect ratio $M/d$ are exogenous. Transposability enters as the coherence, through which differentiation along one axis raises the resource-weighted variance along another (\cref{sm:closedpool}). For a deployed multi-agent system, the environment is the platform, so the repertoire and its seeding are controls (\cref{app:control}).

\Cref{eq:agent,eq:resource,eq:schema} together form the minimal social technology needed to close the perception-action-reward loop (\cref{fig:loop}a) in a way sufficient for social roles to emerge. The resulting multi-agent system dynamics formalizes
Sewell's vision and generates duality as a self-consistent fixed-point equation: schemas shape the role structure, roles generate resources, and resource-weighted structure ($\Cr$) selects schemas. The resource weighting makes survival contingent on success: a schema whose axis carries differentiation among agents who profit by it grows, while one whose adherents gain nothing decays at rate $\kappa$ however much behavior varies along that axis (\cref{sm:fixedpoint}).
The solution set of this equation depends on the parameters and does not generically produce roles. Thus, in the next section we determine for a given game repertoire the nature and location of the phase transition across which role-based society emerges.

\section{Parametrized ignition}

By linearizing \cref{eq:agent-mf} about the symmetric state, in this section we show that the expected social feedback to the identity is $\beta(1-\rhopair)\,\G\,\w_i$ with gain matrix $\G = 2\pi'(0)\, \bm{P}^{\!\top}\!\bm{P}$, where $\bm{P}$ stacks the schema vectors $\g_m$ and $(1-\rhopair)$ is the pairing gain factor---set by the matching scheme's partner correlation $\rhopair$ (defined below), equal to $1$ for random matching and up to $2$ for status-mirrored. The symmetric state destabilizes along eigenvector $k$ of $\G$ when $\beta$ exceeds
\begin{equation}
\beta_c^{(k)} = \frac{\gamma}{(1-\rhopair)\mu_k} ,
\label{eq:cascade}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_1\ge\cdots\ge\mu_R>0$ are the non-zero eigenvalues of $\G$ ($R=\mathrm{rank}(\bm{P})$): role axes activate one at a time as the feedback strength grows. We make the population covariance $\C=N^{-1}\sum_i \w_i\w_i^{\!\top}$ the order parameter.
Its eigenvalues $\lambda_k$ leave the noise floor $\sdyn^2/2\gamma$ one at a time. The number that have crossed, the height of the cascade $N(\beta)$, is the \textit{cultural thickness}: the count of active (supra noise floor) role dimensions.

For a single axis the ignition condition $\beta\, G > \gamma$ is a loop-gain criterion which factorizes as
\begin{equation}
\Lambda \equiv \underbrace{\frac{2\beta}{\gamma}}_{\substack{\text{identity}\\\text{persistence}}}\cdot
\underbrace{\etaag}_{\substack{\text{cognitive}\\\text{capacity}}}\cdot
\underbrace{\frac{1-\rhopair}{\sobs}}_{\substack{\text{channel}\\\text{fidelity}}} > 1,
\label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
where $\etaag \equiv \sobs\,\pi'(0)$ is the agent's dimensionless inference efficiency ($1/\sqrt{2\pi}$ for a threshold agent, $\sqrt{T/2\pi}$ with memory $T$) and $\rhopair\in[-1,1]$ is the correlation the matching scheme induces between a partner's status and one's own (at fixed context $m$). The pairing enters the loop gain only through the factor $(1-\rhopair)$. Independent (random) pairing gives $\rhopair=0$, factor $1$---the reference, recovering the mean-field threshold $\beta_c=\gamma/\mu$; rank-ordered (status-mirrored) matching drives $\rhopair\to-1$ in the population limit, factor $2$, halving the threshold; assortative (homophilic) matching gives $\rhopair\to1$, factor $0$ (\smref{S2}). The correlation needs no pre-existing role modes. $\rhopair$ is a property of the matching algorithm applied to whatever status distribution currently exists, including a unimodal and arbitrarily narrow one, so it is as well defined below threshold, where it sets $\beta_c$, as above it.

The engine runs on two integrators---a write path ($\beta/\gamma$: decisions accumulated into persistent identity) and a read path ($\etaag$: social signals accumulated into posterior inference)---coupled by the information channel between agents. The factors compensate at linear order: well-designed matching can compensate for limited-inference agents and vice versa (similar to the measurement-quality bound on engine performance derived in \cite{Castro2026}). However, even at the best (rank-ordered) matching ($\rhopair\to-1$) ignition requires $\etaag > \sobs\gamma/4\beta$, an \emph{irreducible cognitive floor} below which no interaction orchestration can sustain role structure. At threshold where linear order is accurate, only the product $\Lambda$ is physical. Beyond this threshold the degeneracy of the factorization breaks: $\beta$ sets how far the roles separate, independently of $\rhopair$, while $\rhopair$ sets how often that separation converts to payoff. Role societies at equal $\Lambda$ but different splits are therefore dynamically distinguishable (\smref{S2}). \Cref{fig:loop}b shows the first three bifurcations in the cascade: equilibrating the population at each $\beta$, role axes leave the noise floor one at a time around their critical coupling strengths $\beta_c^{(k)}=\gamma/[(1-\rhopair)\mu_k]$.


\subsection{The gain matrix and the cascade of critical couplings}\label{sm:gain}

We now linearize the mean-field drift \cref{eq:agent-mf} about the symmetric state, showing how the gain matrix $\G=2\pi'(0)\bm{P}^{\!\top}\!\bm{P}$ arises. Its feedback term is $\beta\sum_m f_m\langle a\rangle_m\g_m$, with $\langle a\rangle_m(\w_i)$ the expected signed action in context $m$, averaged over the observation noise and the drawn partner (\cref{sec:model}).

\paragraph{Mean field.} As $N\to\infty$ the partner projection $\w_j\cdot\g_m$ is replaced by its population mean, which vanishes at the symmetric fixed point, so $\Delta_{im}(j)\to\Delta_{im}\equiv\w_i\cdot\g_m$, $s_{im}\approx\Delta_{im}+\sobs\xi_{im}$, and, averaging the decision over the observation noise,
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-abar}
\langle a\rangle_m(\w_i)=2\pi(\Delta_{im})-1,
\end{equation}
which for the naive policy is $\operatorname{erf}\!\bigl(\Delta_{im}/(\sobs\sqrt2)\bigr)$.

\paragraph{Linearization.} At $\w_i=\bm0$ each projection vanishes and $2\pi(0)-1=0$ by policy symmetry. To first order $\langle a\rangle_m\approx 2\pi'(0)(\g_m^{\!\top}\w_i)$, so the feedback term of \cref{eq:agent-mf} becomes
\begin{equation}
\beta\sum_m f_m\langle a\rangle_m\g_m\approx 2\beta\pi'(0)\Bigl(\sum_m f_m\,\g_m\g_m^{\!\top}\Bigr)\w_i=\beta\,\G\,\w_i,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\G\equiv 2\pi'(0)\sum_m f_m\,\g_m\g_m^{\!\top}=2\pi'(0)\,\bm{P}^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{f})\,\bm{P},
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{f}=(f_1,\ldots,f_M)$ and $\bm{P}$ the $M\times d$ matrix of stacked context vectors. We take the play frequencies uniform, $f_m=1/M$, and absorb the constant $1/M$ into $\beta$, so that $\G=2\pi'(0)\,\bm{P}^{\!\top}\bm{P}$ and $\beta$ is the per-context write rate. The frequencies are retained explicitly only where they act as a design lever (\smref{S2\,D}). They are properties of the environment, fixed over the full set of $M$ candidate contexts, and are not renormalized as schemas go extinct. Combined with the linearized restoring force (the cubic term vanishes at the origin),
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-linear}
\dot{\w}_i\approx(-\gamma I+\beta\G)\w_i+\sdyn\bm\eta_i.
\end{equation}
The symmetric state loses stability along eigenvector $k$ of $\G$ when $\beta$ exceeds $\gamma/[(1-\rhopair)\mu_k]$ (\cref{eq:cascade}). Absorbing the constant $(1-\rhopair)$ into $\beta$ as above,
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-cascade}
\beta_c^{(k)}=\gamma/\mu_k,\qquad \mu_1\ge\mu_2\ge\cdots\ge\mu_d,
\end{equation}
the cascade of critical couplings.
%\emph{Scalar check:} $M=1$, $g_1=1$ gives $\G=2\pi'(0)$, $\beta_c=\gamma/2\pi'(0)=\gamma\sobs\sqrt{2\pi}/2$ for the naive policy---the Model~3 result.

\emph{Validity of the linearization.} The subcritical state is not the point $\w=0$ but a Gaussian cloud of finite width about it (covariance $\C$). Stability, however, is a Jacobian property evaluated \emph{at} the fixed point, independent of where the probability mass sits, so \cref{SM-eq:sm-cascade} is the exact stability boundary at any noise level. The cloud's width enters only through the partner average, which softens the gain and shifts onset by $O(\sdyn^2)$. This is a mean-field Ginzburg correction that vanishes with $\sdyn$ and is negligible outside a shrinking critical window (\smref{S6}).

\section{The functional form of the cascade}\label{sm:cascade}

\begin{figure*}[t!]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_cascade_classes}

We assume role-based societies emerge from a ramping of feedback strength with time, summarizing the culture and technology innovations that improve the fidelity of inter-agent communication. Under such ramping, the form of the cascade is a property of the context repertoire alone, which in this section we take as given in full---strengths as well as directions. The ramp's functional form does not enter here, since $N$ is a function of $\beta$. It is fixed only in the monitoring analysis (\cref{sec:monitoring}), where time appears explicitly. The ramp must satisfy a separation of clocks, $\kappa\ll\dot\beta/\beta\ll\gamma$: fast compared with the schema dynamics, so the spectrum is quasi-static across a sweep and the cascade class is well defined, and slow compared with agent relaxation, so the population tracks it. The number of active axes at coupling $\beta$ is the counting function $N(\beta)$. Here, we derive the effective local rate of growth of this function as that for the eigenvalue spectrum $\rho$ in the limit $M,d\to\infty$ at fixed aspect ratio $M/d$, $-d\ln\rho/d\ln\mu$. We find that only three repertoire properties shape $N(\beta)$: the strength hierarchy $\{u_m\}$, the coherence $\{\hat\g_m\cdot\hat\g_n\}$, and the aspect ratio $M/d$. These generate five classes (\cref{fig:classes}): degenerate (single avalanche, no warning), geometric (constant rate), Zipf with exponent $a\gtrless 1$ (decelerating versus runaway acceleration), random Mar\v{c}enko--Pastur bulk (square-root-slow onset, then burst) \cite{marcenko1967}, and spiked (early bifurcation, quiescent interval, avalanche). \Cref{fig:classes}a gives an example of each for $M=50$, while we show the rate for $M\to\infty$ in \cref{fig:classes}b.


\subsection{The counting function and acceleration criterion}

Let $\mu_1\ge\cdots\ge\mu_R>0$ be the nonzero eigenvalues of $\G$, $R=\mathrm{rank}(\bm{P})\le\min(M,d)$. Axis $k$ bifurcates at $\beta_c^{(k)}=\gamma/[(1-\rhopair)\mu_k]$ (\cref{eq:cascade}). Absorbing the fixed factor $(1-\rhopair)$ into $\beta$ for this section, as with the frequencies above, this is $\beta_c^{(k)}=\gamma/\mu_k$. The number of active axes at coupling $\beta$ is
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-counting}
N(\beta)=\#\{k:\mu_k>\gamma/\beta\}=R\,\bar F(\gamma/\beta),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\bar F(\mu)=\int_\mu^\infty\rho(\mu')\,d\mu',
\end{equation}
with $\rho$ the spectral density of $\G$: the cascade is the pushforward of the spectrum under $\mu\mapsto\gamma/\beta$. The rate is
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-rate}
\frac{dN}{d\beta}=R\frac{\gamma}{\beta^2}\rho\!\Bigl(\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\Bigr),\qquad
\frac{dN}{d\ln\beta}=R\,\mu\rho(\mu)\big|_{\mu=\gamma/\beta}.
\end{equation}
Defining the local spectral exponent $\zeta(\mu)\equiv-d\ln\rho/d\ln\mu$, differentiating again gives the acceleration criterion
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-accel}
\frac{d^2N}{d\beta^2}>0\iff\frac{d}{d\mu}[\mu^2\rho(\mu)]<0\iff \zeta>2 \ \text{at}\ \mu=\gamma/\beta.
\end{equation}
A monitor extrapolating a low early rate implicitly assumes $\zeta\le2$ in the unreached part of the spectrum. Whether that holds is a computable property of the repertoire. Only three repertoire properties shape $\rho$: the strength hierarchy $\{u_m\}$, the coherence $\{\hat\g_m\cdot\hat\g_n\}$, and the aspect ratio $q=M/d$.

\subsection{The five exogenous classes}

\cref{tab:classes} catalogs the qualitatively distinct spectral classes and the cascade each induces. Any repertoire's spectrum is locally one of these, and a general cascade concatenates them. Each class is the canonical form taken when one of the three properties dominates. The strength hierarchy $\{u_m\}$ alone sets $\mu_k$ when contexts are effectively orthogonal, and its functional form---flat, exponential, or power-law---distinguishes classes (i)--(iii). The aspect ratio $q=M/d$ alone shapes class (iv), whose Mar\v{c}enko--Pastur edges are functions of $q$. The coherence $\{\hat\g_m\cdot\hat\g_n\}$, through its uniform value $\chi$, produces class (v)'s spike-plus-bulk split, which a strongly condensed strength hierarchy also produces at zero coherence.

The boundary $a=1$ ($\zeta=2$) separates the decelerating and accelerating Zipf regimes. The random class \cite{marcenko1967} has its spectrum confined to $[\mu_-,\mu_+]$, with the edges set by the aspect ratio $q=M/d$. Bifurcations run from the largest eigenvalue down, so the cascade enters the bulk at its upper edge, where the density vanishes as $\rho\sim\sqrt{\mu_+-\mu}$---hence the vanishing onset rate $dN/d\beta\propto(\beta-\beta_c)^{1/2}$ before the bulk. The spiked class, from redundancy (coherent contexts sharing directions), gives one outlier $\mu_{\mathrm{sp}}=|g|^2(1+(M-1)\chi)$ over a degenerate bulk $\mu_\perp=|g|^2(1-\chi)$ at uniform coherence $\chi$, hence a quiescent interval of relative width $(1+(M-1)\chi)/(1-\chi)$ and then an avalanche. Rate data alone cannot distinguish this interval from a cascade that has ended.

\section{Schema-Resource dynamics select the cascade class}

\noindent In the previous section, the schema strengths, and thus the cascade type (\cref{fig:classes}), were taken as given. In the model they emerge endogenously from the stationary solution of the schema-resource dynamics. The directions stay exogenous throughout (\cref{sec:model}). Here, we analyze that solution space through the lens of the resulting cascade type to arrive at a diagram denoting which regimes of schema-resource dynamics exhibit which cascades types across the transition to a role-based society. In particular, we take the $M$ contexts as an ambient pool of candidate schemas and ask how many survive and with what strengths. The active set is those with $u_m=|\g_m|^2$ above the seed scale at which schemas are initialized, an emergent number $K\le M$. The threshold is bookkeeping for the transient: at the fixed point the inactive schemas have decayed to zero and $K$ is set by $\alpha\lambda_m\ge\kappa$ alone (\cref{SM-eq:sm-Meff}). Rewriting \cref{eq:schema}, strengths grow by the resource-weighted variance along each axis, $\tfrac{d}{dt}\ln u_m = 2(\alpha\lambda_m(\Cr)-\kappa)$. Imposing a finite social bandwidth $\sum_m u_m\le S_{\mathrm{bw}}$---the bounded collective attention the population can spend legibly signaling status across all active schemas at once---bounds the total. The shares follow a replicator dynamics with per-schema fitness $\alpha\lambda_m$ \cite{touzel2026fitnesslandscapesocialnorms,Gintis2010}, generated by the engine chain: \textit{schema $\to$ differentiation $\to$ signal $\to$ coordination $\to$ resources}.


A Gaussian mean-field evaluation (\cref{sm:closedpool}) reduces this competition to a single interior fixed point that is a saddle (\cref{sm:saddle}). It is the \emph{degenerate} state---all active schemas at one strength: a schema is stationary only at a common invasion fitness (\cref{sm:closedpool}), and because fitness rises with a schema's own strength, equal fitness forces equal strength (a \emph{pinning} condition, \cref{sm:closedpool}). The state is stable in total magnitude, since an imposed capacity ceiling (\cref{sm:closedpool}) regulates the sum, but unstable in \emph{composition}. Strength can shift between schemas, and the one that gains is now fitter, so it grows at the expense of the rest. This \emph{winner-take-all} instability diverges at each bifurcation. The dynamics leave the saddle one axis at a time. An inactive axis sits with its covariance eigenvalue pinned at the noise floor $\sdyn^2/2\gamma$. When an axis activates, the resources it generates reweight $\Cr$ toward the agents who differentiated along it. Because those agents also spread in other directions, this inflates the residual variance off the active axis, and the next schema grows until its eigenvalue clears the floor and ignites. Successive axes leave the floor in turn---this is the endogenous cascade.


What are the properties of the replicator dynamics that control which cascade type is exhibited? We consider transient and stationary components in turn.
First, the transient, which we call the \emph{sort}: the seeded pool of candidates is winnowed and ordered into a strength hierarchy en route to the stationary state. It has two emergent rates, an \emph{activation rate} $\ract$ at which successive axes cross threshold and a \emph{compounding rate} $g$ at which an active schema's strength grows. Both are outputs of the dynamics, not control knobs. Their ratio $r=\ract/g$ counts the axes that cross threshold per \emph{compounding time} $1/g$, the time an active schema takes to grow by a factor $e$. It is set by the dispersion of the seeded repertoire (\cref{sm:closedpool}), that is, by how unequally the candidate contexts are weighted at the outset. The stationary dynamics has four dimensionless quantities, but only one is needed to span the morphology between regimes (the rest set a fixed backdrop). The ratio $\Theta=M/M_{\mathrm{cap}}$ is the number of schemas competing per capacity slot, where the \emph{capacity} $M_{\mathrm{cap}}=S_{\mathrm{bw}}/u_{\mathrm{sat}}$ counts how many schemas the finite bandwidth $S_{\mathrm{bw}}$ sustains at the per-schema ceiling $u_{\mathrm{sat}}$. Large $\Theta$ divides the budget more finely, so less of it reaches any one schema. Two independent limits fix the capacity: a per-schema resolution ceiling $u_{\mathrm{sat}}$ and the total bandwidth $S_{\mathrm{bw}}$. $\Theta$ measures which is reached first. Below one, the per-schema ceiling caps every schema and all survive; above one, the shared budget runs out first and only $M_{\mathrm{cap}}$ do. Only this ratio is physical, so we parametrize by it rather than an absolute $u_{\mathrm{sat}}$ (\cref{sm:closedpool}). Thus, the principal $(\Theta,r)$ slice (\cref{fig:regimes}) is an effective phase diagram in two dimensions: the capacity a society can sustain (\cref{fig:regimes}a) and the thickness it realizes (\cref{fig:regimes}b). The two coordinates act on different objects. $\Theta$ selects the stationary endpoint: the saturation ceiling caps the condensation instability when capacity exceeds demand ($\Theta<1$, giving a degenerate spectral cluster), and leaves that instability unchecked when the budget cannot sustain even one schema ($\Theta>M$, giving a spiked spectrum). Its boundaries are therefore sharp phase lines. $r$ measures how far apart in time successive candidates cross threshold. When the seeded repertoire is widely dispersed, the earliest contexts compound longest and incumbents entrench; when it is narrow, candidates ignite together and churn instead. The two limits are a smooth crossover between the geometric and Zipf cascades.


\subsection{The self-consistent fixed point}\label{sm:fixedpoint}
% ======================================================================

Recalling the coupled system (\cref{eq:agent-mf,eq:resource,eq:schema}) and the near-threshold linearization of \cref{sm:gain}, we now solve for the self-consistent stationary state that closes the loop. The three dynamics evolve on separated timescales $\gamma\gg c\gg\kappa$ (agents equilibrate, then resources, then schemas):
\begin{align}
\dot{\w}_i&=-\gamma\w_i(1+|\w_i|^2)+\beta\sum_mf_m \langle a\rangle_m\g_m+\sdyn\bm\eta_i,\nonumber\\
\dot r_i&=\sum_m f_m\,\mathbb{E}_{j\sim\mathcal P_i}[W_{ijm}]-c\,r_i,\\
\dot u_m&=2u_m(\alpha\lambda_m-\kappa).\nonumber
\end{align}

\paragraph{Step 1: agents.} At fixed schemas the drift is a gradient, $\bm v=-\nabla_{\w}E$, with
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-energy}
\begin{gathered}
E(\w)=\gamma\Bigl(\tfrac{|\w|^2}{2}+\tfrac{|\w|^4}{4}\Bigr)-\beta\sum_m\Psi(\w\cdot\g_m),\\[2pt]
\Psi'(z)=2\pi(z)-1,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
so the stationary distribution is Boltzmann, $p^*(\w)\propto\exp(-2E(\w)/\sdyn^2)$, and agents are i.i.d.\ draws. As in \cref{sm:gain}, linearizing near the symmetric state (dropping the quartic and nonlinear terms) gives drift $(-\gamma I+\beta\G)\w_i$, so near threshold $p^*$ is Gaussian with covariance (Lyapunov equation, symmetric drift)
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-C-linear}
\C=\frac{\sdyn^2}{2}(\gamma I-\beta\G)^{-1},
\end{equation}
valid for $\beta<\beta_c^{(1)}$. $\C$ diverges along the leading eigenvector of $\G$ at the bifurcation. Each covariance eigenvalue $\lambda_k=\sdyn^2/2(\gamma-\beta\mu_k)$ increases with the gain $\mu_k$ it comes from, so \cref{SM-eq:sm-C-linear} can be inverted: measuring the covariance spectrum below threshold recovers $\{\mu_k\}$, and with it the cascade still to come (\cref{sec:monitoring}).

\paragraph{Step 2: resources.} At steady state $r_i^*=c^{-1}\sum_m f_m\,\mathbb{E}_{j}[W_{ijm}]$, a deterministic function of $\w_i$. Near threshold the error rate linearizes, $P_e\approx\tfrac12-|(\w-\w')\cdot\g_m|\pi'(0)$, so $W_{ijm}\approx 4w_0\pi'(0)^2((\w_i-\w_j)\cdot\g_m)^2$. Averaging over partners ($\mathbb{E}_j[\w_j]=0$, $\mathbb{E}_j[(\w_j\cdot\g_m)^2]=\g_m^{\!\top}\C\g_m$),
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-rstar}
r^*(\w)\approx\frac{4w_0\pi'(0)^2}{c}\sum_m f_m\Bigl((\w\cdot\g_m)^2+\g_m^{\!\top}\C\g_m\Bigr).
\end{equation}
The first term is agent-specific; the second a population baseline.

\paragraph{Step 3: resource-weighted covariance.} With $\Cr=\int r^*(\w)\w\w^{\!\top}p^*/\int r^*(\w)p^*$ and $r^*$ quadratic in $\w$, the numerator is a fourth moment, evaluated for Gaussian $p^*$ by Wick's theorem (Isserlis' theorem in the statistics literature),
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-isserlis}
\mathbb{E}[(\w\cdot\g_m)^2\,\omega_a\omega_b]=(\g_m^{\!\top}\C\g_m)\,C_{ab}+2(\g_m^{\!\top}\C)_a(\g_m^{\!\top}\C)_b,
\end{equation}
making $\Cr$ a function of $\C$ and $\{\g_m\}$ alone. Even when $\C\propto I$, $\Cr$ is anisotropic, because $r^*(\w)\propto\sum_m f_m(\w\cdot\g_m)^2$ weights agents aligned with the context directions more heavily. This anisotropy resolves the collapse of the unweighted dynamics ($r_i\equiv1$, $\Cr=\C$): $\Cr$ inherits structure from the repertoire even when the population is isotropic.

\paragraph{Step 4: schemas.} At steady state each surviving schema satisfies $\alpha\lambda_m=\kappa$, with $\lambda_m=\hat\g_m^{\!\top}\Cr\hat\g_m$ the resource-weighted variance along its (fixed) axis. Those with $\lambda_m<\kappa/\alpha$ decay to the seed scale.

\paragraph{The loop and multiplicity.} The conditions close: $\{\g_m\}\!\to\! E\!\to\! p^*\!\to\!\C\!\to\! r^*\!\to\!\Cr\!\to\!\{\g_m\}$. The trivial solution $\g_m^*=0$ always exists. Nontrivial solutions appear when an eigenvalue of the (already anisotropic) $\Cr$ exceeds $\kappa/\alpha$. Since $\Cr$ has $d$ eigenvalues, the sustained schema count is
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-Meff}
M_{\mathrm{eff}}=\#\{k:\lambda_k(\Cr)\ge\kappa/\alpha\}\le d,
\end{equation}
the endogenous cultural thickness. Without resources ($r_i\equiv1$, $\Cr=\C$) the $\lambda_m$ rank the fixed axes by their alignment with the leading eigenvector of $\C$, so the survivor set narrows toward the best-aligned schema. The resource reweighting, anisotropic along the repertoire directions themselves, lifts several $\lambda_m$ over $\kappa/\alpha$ at once and sustains a multi-schema state. \Cref{SM-eq:sm-Meff} is the survivor count $K\le M$ used in the closed-pool construction of \cref{sm:closedpool}, an identification that requires distinct survivors to occupy distinct eigen-directions of $\Cr$. Fixed axes secure it: a schema cannot rotate onto another, so two survivors share a direction only if the repertoire supplies them one. Under the vector dynamics of \cref{app:directions} the counts would instead separate, since every schema rotates toward the same leading eigenvector.

\subsection{The closed-pool (survivor) construction}\label{sm:closedpool}

Under the full loop the spectrum is not an input but a prediction. We treat the $M$ context directions as candidate schemas and ask how many survive. The active set is $\{m:u_m>u_0\}$ with $u_m=|\g_m|^2$ and $u_0$ the seed scale, the small strength at which schemas are seeded and above which they count as active. Its size $K\le M$ is the emergent survivor count \cref{SM-eq:sm-Meff}. The threshold reads off the transient count only, since inactive schemas decay to zero. But $u_0$ does enter the regime boundaries through the log dynamic range $L=\ln(u_{\mathrm{sat}}/u_0)$ of \cref{sm:regimes}. Adding to \cref{eq:schema} the finite legible-status bandwidth $S_{\mathrm{bw}}$ shared across schemas, the strengths obey
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-strength}
\frac{d}{dt}\ln u_m=2\bigl(\alpha\lambda_m(\Cr)-\kappa-\varphi(U)\bigr),\qquad U=\sum_n u_n,
\end{equation}
a replicator dynamics on the shares with per-schema fitness $\alpha\lambda_m$---the total reinforcement carried by schema $m$ being $F_m=\g_m^{\!\top}\Cr\g_m=u_m\lambda_m$---and a soft budget pressure $\varphi(U)$ shared by every schema: negligible while the channel is slack ($U\ll S_{\mathrm{bw}}$, $\varphi\approx0$) and rising to cap the total as it fills ($\varphi'>0$, $\varphi\to\infty$ as $U\to S_{\mathrm{bw}}$). The bare $\kappa$-only dynamics ($\varphi\equiv0$) leaves the total unregulated---since $\lambda_m$ grows with $u_m$ (below), a schema past the invasion strength would grow without bound. $\varphi$ supplies the missing restoring force, equally on every axis, so it shifts the invasion threshold without biasing which schemas survive. The budget is a carrying capacity, not a conservation law: attention is capped rather than conserved and redistributed, so it can be left under-used. Keeping $\kappa$ a fixed decay rate and the budget a soft inequality ($U\le S_{\mathrm{bw}}$), rather than passing to the normalized replicator, preserves two regimes that pinning the total would eliminate. The first is a sub-threshold state in which every schema decays, the pre-role condition the ignition onset transitions out of. The second is a slack regime ($\Theta<1$, $U<S_{\mathrm{bw}}$) in which all $M$ schemas coexist at the per-schema ceiling, the durable multiplicity central to the Sewellian picture. A single winner emerges not from relative selection, which admits interior coexistence, but from the increase of $\lambda_m$ with $u_m$ established below.

\paragraph{The pinning saddle.} Summing \cref{SM-eq:sm-isserlis} over contexts gives a closed form for the resource-weighted covariance, independent of repertoire geometry,
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-Cr-closed}
\Cr=\C+\frac{\C\,\G\,\C}{\operatorname{tr}(\C\G)}.
\end{equation}
Since $\C=\tfrac{\sdyn^2}{2}(\gamma I-\beta\G)^{-1}$ commutes with $\G$, $\Cr$ shares the gain eigenbasis. For orthogonal contexts \cref{SM-eq:sm-Cr-closed} collapses to a scalar relation on each axis,
\begin{align}\label{SM-eq:sm-lambda-m}
\lambda_m=v_m\Bigl(1+\frac{u_mv_m}{S}\Bigr),\quad \mathrm{with}\\
v_m=\frac{\sdyn^2}{2(\gamma-2\beta\pi'(0)u_m)}\quad \mathrm{and}\quad S\equiv\sum_n u_nv_n. \nonumber
\end{align}

Two competing results follow.

\emph{The pinning condition.} A schema is stationary only where its replicator rate \cref{SM-eq:sm-strength} vanishes, $\alpha\lambda_m=\kappa+\varphi^\ast$ with $\varphi^\ast=\varphi(U^\ast)$ the equilibrium budget pressure, so at an interior fixed point all survivors share one fitness $\lambda_m=(\kappa+\varphi^\ast)/\alpha$. The shift $\varphi^\ast$ is common to every axis, so equal fitness still demands equal strength. $\lambda_m$ is strictly increasing in $u_m$: both factors in \cref{SM-eq:sm-lambda-m} grow with $u_m$ across the sub-critical range $u_m<\gamma/2\beta\pi'(0)$, a property that extends to non-orthogonal repertoires (\smref{S5}). The interior fixed point therefore carries a degenerate spectrum. This is the competitive-exclusion result for replicators, sharpened by monotonicity from equal fitness to equal strength.

\emph{The condensation instability.} The pinned point is a saddle, and both of its directions follow from \cref{SM-eq:sm-strength} directly. Along the \emph{magnitude} direction $u_m=u+\delta$ (all schemas together), growth is arrested by whichever capacity ceiling is reached first (\cref{sm:regimes}): the per-schema cap $u_{\mathrm{sat}}$, or the total budget through $\varphi(U)$, which subtracts a restoring $2M\varphi'(U^\ast)\,\delta$ from every growth rate against the destabilizing $2\alpha\,(\partial\lambda/\partial u)\,\delta$. This stabilizes the direction once $M\varphi'(U^\ast)>\alpha\,\partial\lambda/\partial u$. Either way an imposed ceiling supplies the restoring force that pins the total; the bare dynamics do not. Along a \emph{composition} direction $\sum_m\delta u_m=0$ the total, and hence $\varphi$, are unchanged, and \cref{SM-eq:sm-strength} linearizes to $\dot{\delta u}_m\propto\delta\lambda_m\propto\delta u_m-\overline{\delta u}$: because $\lambda_m$ increases with $u_m$, any schema pushed above the mean gains fitness and grows, draining the rest---\emph{unstable}. One stable magnitude direction and $M-1$ unstable composition directions make the equal-strength state a saddle (\cref{sm:saddle}), and the dynamics leave it toward condensation. The departure is autocatalytic near a bifurcation, where $v_m$ (and hence $\partial\lambda_m/\partial u_m$) diverges. There the budget pressure arrests the runaway at a saturated cluster, and so regulates condensation. Whether it does so (a cluster) or does not (a bare condensate) is set by $\Theta=Mu_{\mathrm{sat}}/S_{\mathrm{bw}}$, mapped globally in \cref{fig:regimes} above.

\begin{figure}[t!]

\paragraph{Sequential sort and the two emergent rates.} The candidate schemas do not cross threshold simultaneously: near threshold the $\lambda_m$ of the growing cohort are nearly equal, so the seeded spread in $u_m$ is carried at a common rate into a staggered sequence of threshold crossings. Two rates characterize the transient. The \emph{compounding rate} is read from \cref{SM-eq:sm-strength} for an active, pre-saturation schema (where the channel is slack and $\varphi\approx0$),
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-g}
g\equiv 2(\alpha\lambda_\star-\kappa),
\end{equation}
$\lambda_\star$ the common resource-weighted variance of the growing cohort. $g$ is set by the gain-spectrum gap at the growing axis. The \emph{activation rate} $\ract$ is the rate at which successive survivors cross threshold. Candidates seeded at $u_m(0)=u_0e^{\sseed z_m}$, with $\sseed$ the log-dispersion of the seeded repertoire and $z_m$ standard normal, compound at the common rate $g$ and so reach a fixed threshold at times spread by $\Delta\ln u/g$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-nu}
\ract\propto g/\sseed,
\end{equation}
The readout $r\equiv\ract/g\propto1/\sseed$ is therefore fixed by the dispersion of the candidate repertoire and is not set independently. Both $\ract$ and $g$ are emergent. $\sseed$ is a property of the repertoire, which for a deployed system is operator-held (\cref{app:control}).

\paragraph{Classification of stationary states.} At a fixed point of
\cref{SM-eq:sm-strength} every schema is extinct ($u_m=0$, $\alpha\lambda_m<\kappa+\varphi^\ast$),
marginal ($0<u_m<u_{\mathrm{sat}}$, $\alpha\lambda_m=\kappa+\varphi^\ast$, at the invasion
threshold), or saturated ($u_m=u_{\mathrm{sat}}$). Since pinning forces equal
fitness to equal strength, no stationary state carries a graded active
distribution, so there are exactly two fixed-point families. (a)~The
\emph{degenerate} state: all survivors equal, stabilized by the ceiling into a cluster of $\min(M,M_{\mathrm{cap}})$ saturated schemas. Its flat spectrum bifurcates as a single avalanche (class~i). (b)~The \emph{spiked} state: one
condensate carrying the budget with a fringe pinned at the invasion margin,
reached when the budget cannot cap even one schema (class~v). $\Theta$ exchanges them---the degenerate cluster shrinks from $M$ schemas
($\Theta\le1$) to $M_{\mathrm{cap}}$ ($1<\Theta\le M$) to one ($\Theta>M$, the
spike). The geometric and Zipf classes are \emph{not} fixed points but
morphologies of the sequential sort toward these endpoints, and are the
observable object because a monitor watches roles form. Slow age-ordered relaxation is geometric (class~ii, $k_0=\ract/g$), a share-proportional sort under a budget at capacity is Yule--Simon and hence Zipf (class~iii, $a\sim g/\ract$), and an unorganized exogenous repertoire is Marchenko--Pastur (class~iv). The
replicator thus selects the cascade in two steps: $\Theta$ fixes the endpoint,
and $r=\ract/g$ fixes the transient morphology and its
lifetime---no spectrum is imposed.

\subsection{The regime map and its exact boundaries}\label{sm:regimes}

The cascade morphology depends on $\sim5$ dimensionless groups, but only two separate regimes: the schemas per capacity slot $\Theta=M/M_{\mathrm{cap}}$ and the activations per compounding time $r=\ract/g$. Both rates are emergent, so $r$ is a readout rather than a knob. It is fixed by the repertoire's seed dispersion through \cref{SM-eq:sm-nu}, which the operator holds wherever it defines the context set (\smref{S4\,A}). Low $r$ is a widely dispersed repertoire, whose candidates cross threshold well separated in time; high $r$ a narrow one, whose candidates ignite together. Here $M_{\mathrm{cap}}\equiv S_{\mathrm{bw}}/u_{\mathrm{sat}}$ is the schema capacity, the number of fully saturated schemas the budget admits, so $\Theta=Mu_{\mathrm{sat}}/S_{\mathrm{bw}}$ counts demand $M$ against capacity $M_{\mathrm{cap}}$. It is distinct from the realized survivor count $K$, since survivors need not be saturated. The two also have different ranges: $M_{\mathrm{cap}}$ exceeds $M$ in the slack regime ($\Theta<1$) and drops below $1$ in the winner-take-all regime ($\Theta>M$), whereas $K\le d$. Neither ceiling is intrinsic: the bare reinforcement supplies no turnover of its own. Along a single active axis the thick-phase strength is $\lambda(u)=\beta k u/\gamma+\sdyn^2/4(\beta k u-\gamma)-1$ (condensate amplitude plus fluctuation correction, with $\mu=ku$), which grows without turning over: $d^2\lambda/du^2=\beta^2k^2\sdyn^2/2(\beta ku-\gamma)^3>0$ throughout the supercritical range, so no \emph{intrinsic} strength exists at which reinforcement saturates. The quartic confinement of \cref{eq:agent-mf} caps the differentiation \emph{amplitude} ($m^2=\beta\mu/\gamma-1$) but not the schema \emph{strength}, which keeps rising as the two role modes separate. Two imposed ceilings arrest it, and they are distinct: a per-schema cap $u_{\mathrm{sat}}$, and the total budget $S_{\mathrm{bw}}$ enforced by the soft pressure $\varphi(U)$ of \cref{SM-eq:sm-strength}. Their ratio $\Theta=Mu_{\mathrm{sat}}/S_{\mathrm{bw}}$ fixes which is reached first. We take $u_{\mathrm{sat}}$ schema-independent. A finite \emph{legible-status channel}---bounded collective attention, the same resource whose noise scale is $\sobs$---grounds both constants. Its total capacity bounds the summed strength, and its per-axis resolution caps any single schema, since an axis cannot be differentiated beyond what the channel resolves. So $u_{\mathrm{sat}}$ is a resolution scale set by $\sobs$ (the measurement-quality limit of Ref.~\cite{Castro2026}) rather than a free parameter, $M_{\mathrm{cap}}$ counts the roles the channel can resolve, and $\Theta$ is demand measured against that count. The channel-to-strength mapping carries a convention, so this fixes $u_{\mathrm{sat}}$ up to proportionality, which is all $\Theta$ requires. Since only the dimensionless ratio $\Theta=Mu_{\mathrm{sat}}/S_{\mathrm{bw}}$ enters the morphology, we parametrize the regime map by this ratio alone, rendered in \cref{fig:regimes} as the capacity $M_{\mathrm{cap}}/M=1/\Theta$, and never fix an absolute $u_{\mathrm{sat}}$. The only absolute scale retained is the seed-to-budget ratio $u_0/S_{\mathrm{bw}}$, which sets the log dynamic range $L=\ln(u_{\mathrm{sat}}/u_0)$ entering the saturated-cluster/geometric boundary. This is the seed \emph{scale}, independent of the seed \emph{dispersion} $\sseed$ that fixes the horizontal axis. The remaining groups ($\alpha\langle\lambda\rangle/\kappa$, supercriticality $\beta\mu_1/\gamma$, aspect ratio $M/d$) set a fixed backdrop, as does the repertoire geometry $\{\hat\g_m\cdot\hat\g_n\}$, which the map takes near-orthogonal. The counting statements hold in that limit: orthogonal axes make the gain spectrum $\{\mu_k=2\pi'(0)u_k\}$, so eigenvalues and strengths coincide and $M_{\mathrm{cap}}$ counts saturated schemas directly. Overlap breaks the identification. Schemas then couple through $\Cr$ as well as through the shared budget, which can be reached before the per-schema ceiling and leave survivors equalized below $u_{\mathrm{sat}}$, so the boundaries degrade into crossovers as the repertoire becomes coherent. The width of that window is not fixed here. \cref{fig:regimes} is the principal $(\Theta,r)$ slice, with $r=\ract/g$ the generative shape parameter fixed by the seed dispersion through \cref{SM-eq:sm-nu}. Four endogenous regimes arise:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Saturated cluster.} Early saturation or hard caps arrest condensation and the pinning condition re-equalizes the winners: near-degenerate spectrum, class (i), single avalanche.
\item \textbf{Geometric.} A widely dispersed repertoire ($r\ll1$) with a standing cohort: strengths encode age, $u_m\propto e^{g(t-t_m)}$, and rank-ordering gives $\mu_k\propto e^{-k/k_0}$, $k_0=\ract/g$---class (ii), the log cascade, best case for monitoring.
\item \textbf{Zipf.} A budget at capacity with share-proportional growth is a Yule--Simon process \cite{simon1955} with $\mu_k\propto k^{-a}$, $a\sim g/\ract$: entrenchment ($g\gg\ract$, wide seed dispersion) decelerates, churn ($\ract\gtrsim g$, narrow dispersion) accelerates.
\item \textbf{Spiked.} Unregulated condensation drives one condensate plus a fringe pinned at the invasion margin $F_m=(\kappa/\alpha)u_m$: class (v), in which one axis activates, a gap follows, and the rest arrive at once---the natural endpoint of unregulated competition.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Exact boundaries.} On the principal slice the boundaries are exact analytic curves:
\begin{align}
\text{budget at capacity:}&\quad \Theta=1, \label{SM-eq:sm-b-theta1}\\
\text{winner-take-all possible:}&\quad \Theta=M, \label{SM-eq:sm-b-thetaM}\\
\text{Zipf $a=1$:}&\quad r=1 \ \ (a=1/r), \label{SM-eq:sm-b-a1}\\
\text{sat.\ cluster$|$geometric:}&\quad r=\frac{M}{L(\Theta)}, \label{SM-eq:sm-b-e1e2}\\
\text{with}&\quad L(\Theta)=\ln\frac{u_{\mathrm{sat}}}{u_0}=\ln\frac{\Theta S_{\mathrm{bw}}}{Mu_0}. \nonumber
\end{align}
These curves are exact in the self-averaged ($N\to\infty$) mean-field limit used throughout. Finite populations fluctuate around them, and \emph{which} schema condenses is a symmetry-breaking choice the class does not fix. The map is in this sense a prediction: the sort, its classes, and these boundaries follow from the near-threshold Gaussian covariance (\cref{sm:closedpool}) extended into the post-bifurcation thick phase, whose full-loop verification is left to the accompanying simulation program.
Boundary \cref{SM-eq:sm-b-e1e2} is where the number of saturation-pinned modes $n_{\mathrm{sat}}=\max(0,M-rL)$ reaches zero: below $r=M/L$ a saturated cluster of $n_{\mathrm{sat}}$ modes coexists with a geometric tail (a mixed avalanche-plus-log class); above it the cluster is gone. This transition lies within the thick capacity band, where \cref{fig:regimes} marks it by the avalanche and logarithmic glyphs rather than a drawn curve. The colour field of \cref{fig:monitor}a is the bifurcation clustering, defined as the maximal share of bifurcation points $b_k=1/\mu_k$ falling within a window $\Delta\ln\beta=0.15$. It is a continuous diagnostic and varies smoothly across these sharp analytic lines. It reaches its floor slightly before \cref{SM-eq:sm-b-e1e2}, because a few residual pinned modes still cluster just below $n_{\mathrm{sat}}=0$.

\paragraph{Two classes are not endogenous fixed points.} The Marchenko--Pastur bulk (iv) is the signature of a repertoire not yet organized by resource feedback (the natural initial condition for freshly seeded schemas, and transient under the loop), and its persistence in data would itself indicate the loop has not closed. The exact degeneracy of the saturated-cluster regime holds only within the Gaussian pinning argument. Generic cross-axis resource coupling and residual coherence broaden it into a narrow cluster.

\paragraph{Transient, not stationary.} The geometric and Zipf classes are morphologies of the transient sort toward the pinned or condensed fixed point, not stationary distributions. Sustaining a hierarchy indefinitely requires continual injection of new schemas (schema innovation)---an open-repertoire process outside the closed loop, which we do not treat. Within the closed loop, the endogenous source of novelty during the sort is transposability, the context overlap $\g_m\cdot\g_n$ through which the developing $\Cr$ raises successive axes to threshold.

What can a monitor watching the first few bifurcations extrapolate about the shape of the cascade?
The theory supplies a population-level diagnostic requiring no interpretability of individual agents: embed behavior, track the eigenspectrum of $\C$. Two results make this practical.
First, monitoring has a speed limit.
Estimating the relaxation time $\tau_k=1/|\gamma-\beta\mu_k|$ requires observing for $O(\tau_k)$, but $\tau_k$ diverges at threshold. This is the critical slowing down whose rise is a generic early-warning signal \cite{scheffer2009}. If the engine parameter is swept at rate $\dot\Lambda$, the dimensionless ratio $\mathcal{R}=\tau_k\dot\Lambda$ controls feasibility. When the forcing is fast relative to the system's internal timescale, the sweep crosses the transition before the slowing can be measured. This is rate-induced tipping, where slowing-down indicators generically fail \cite{ashwin2012,ritchie2016}. Slow deployment ($\mathcal{R}\ll1$) preserves genuine early warning, and fast deployment forecloses it. Second, the spectrum is in principle measurable \emph{before} any bifurcation, which remedies this. Subcritically $\C=\tfrac{\sdyn^2}{2}(\gamma I - \beta\G)^{-1}$, so inverting the empirical eigenvalue distribution of behavioral embeddings recovers $\rho(\mu)$, and with it the full $N(\beta)$, its acceleration profile, and any avalanche masked by an early spike (\cref{fig:monitor}). Rate extrapolation cannot resolve these ambiguities. Two caveats scope this readout. It recovers the spectrum of the \emph{current} repertoire. Because $\G$ itself drifts as schemas and resources evolve, it forecasts the imminent cascade only insofar as that drift is slow relative to onset. The inversion is exact for a fixed repertoire and only a forecast for a changing one. The readout is also a population statistic estimated from finite behavioral samples. Resolving closely spaced eigenvalues against the sampling bulk therefore imposes a finite-sample floor that a deployment analysis must respect, distinct from the dynamical speed limit above. The class structure also makes the cascade designable, but the prescription is not monotone in $r=\ract/g$: it reverses between capacity bands. Where the budget caps growth ($1<\Theta\le M$) the sort is Yule--Simon with exponent $a=1/r$, so a widely dispersed repertoire (lower $r$) steepens the schema-strength hierarchy, each activated schema compounding longer before the next crosses threshold. That spreads the bifurcation thresholds into the extrapolable regime. A narrow repertoire (higher $r$) flattens spectra and moves the system toward the avalanche-prone classes. In the thick band ($\Theta\le1$) the operative boundary is instead $r=M/L(\Theta)$ (\cref{sm:regimes}): below it a saturated cluster ignites at once, so there the preference reverses and it is larger $r$ that resolves the cascade into a sequence. Independently of $r$, per-context pairing efficiencies $\phi_m=1-\rho_m$ reweight the effective spectrum $\bm{P}^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\phi})\,\bm{P}$, letting an operator stretch clustered eigenvalues into a resolvable sequence at some cost in coordination efficiency. These levers also frame a conjecture about deployment, in which parameters $\Theta$ and $r$ (fixed here) might evolve systematically, moving the system toward the avalanche-prone corner of \cref{fig:monitor}: more mature systems sitting at higher $\Theta$ (more coordination competing for finite bandwidth) and, as a platform's task taxonomy fills in around a settled core, at higher $r$ (a narrower spread of context weights). Engineering that improves coordination would then erode its own monitorability. Engineering practice outside the present theory sets the drift of these parameters across a system's lifetime. Making that drift an endogenous trajectory would require promoting $\Theta$ and $r$ to slow dynamical variables, a two-timescale extension we leave open.


\subsection{Consequences for monitoring and design}

The classification converts a low observed rate into a decision procedure. The first few inter-bifurcation spacings identify the class: constant ratios $\beta_{k+1}/\beta_k$ indicate geometric (estimate $k_0$, predict the rest); ratios growing as $((k{+}1)/k)^a$ indicate Zipf (the exponent's position relative to $a=1$ decides decay versus explosion); a rate rising as $(\beta-\beta_c)^{1/2}$ indicates an imminent random bulk. A long quiescent interval is not evidence that the cascade has ended, since it is also the spiked signature. Rate data alone cannot distinguish the two, and the subcritical spectral inversion resolves the ambiguity. The complementary design levers on the cascade class are collected in \cref{app:control}.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Distributional AGI: a control surface.} Recent safety work \cite{tomasev2026} hypothesizes that general-level AI capability may emerge not in one model but from the coordination of many sub-general agents. Our framework makes this quantitative: the thick phase is the coordinated collective. Shared substrates---task queues, codebases, finite compute---make same-action encounters interfere, which gives the payoffs an anti-coordination structure. Complementary roles (planner/executor, coder/reviewer) then extract gains no member could achieve alone, sustained by self-generated signals rather than assigned by an orchestrator. It is this emergent, unassigned case that evades inspection of the system's design. Two consequences bound the distributional AGI hypothesis. First, the transition can be crossed by improving the coordination layer alone---routing, protocols, channel noise ($\rhopair$, $\sobs$)---with no change in any agent, so per-agent capability evaluations will not detect it, and at scale the crossover sharpens toward a discontinuity. Second, the cognitive floor bounds what orchestration can achieve. Agents with $\etaag<\sobs\gamma/4\beta$ cannot ignite regardless of coordination, and the number of sustainable roles is capped by inference quality through the dense-associative-memory bound $M_{\mathrm{eff}}\le d^{\,n-1}$ \cite{krotov2016}, with $n$ (policy sharpness) rising with capability \cite{gershman2015}. Crude agents sustain a coder and a reviewer, not an organization, but the ceiling rises steeply with modest gains (\smeq{S10}).

The same factors that set ignition and shape the cascade are largely \emph{platform-owned}, so an operator who fixes the environment before ignition holds a control surface over which takeoff scenario is reachable. It is a design choice among settings, not control-theoretic steering during takeoff, which the rate-induced-tipping limit forbids (\cref{fig:monitor}). We set out the available levers, their costs, and the two tensions that bound them in \cref{app:control}.

\paragraph{Outlook.} The model realizes Sewell's duality as a fixed-point equation---schema $\leftrightarrow$ context direction, resource $\leftrightarrow$ accumulated coordination payoff, structure $\leftrightarrow$ resource-weighted covariance, agency $\leftrightarrow$ deviation from prescribed role, transposability $\leftrightarrow$ context overlap $\hat\g_m\cdot\hat\g_n$. One object, the spectrum of the social gain matrix, then organizes onset, cascade shape, monitorability, and design. The main scope condition concerns transformation: the strengths $u_m$ are endogenous, the axes $\hat\g_m$ are not. The loop therefore goes beyond reproduction---which schemas govern is itself an outcome---but stops short of transformation in Sewell's sense, which requires a schema to change what it is about. Sewell attributes social novelty to that transformation. Part of that mechanism is already present as the overlap $\hat\g_m\cdot\hat\g_n$, through which differentiation along one axis raises the resource-weighted variance along another. Between orthogonal contexts the coupling is competitive only, through the shared budget. Moving a schema onto a new axis requires the angular sector the closed loop does not evolve, and \cref{app:directions} sets out what an extension of it must decide. Nor does the model capture Sewell's claim that transformation is perpetual: our dynamics sort into roles and settle to a pinned or condensed fixed point, whereas a Sewellian society never settles. Sustained schema multiplicity lies outside the closed loop. We leave it open.

Two extensions remain within the emergence regime. Retaining the role-sign dependence of coordination income, which the mean field averages away, lets the game's payoff asymmetry ($6$ versus $2$) concentrate resources in dominant roles, adding endogenous stratification to differentiation. A mixed repertoire of anti-coordination and conformity games couples the differentiation sector treated here to a consensus sector, with gain eigenvalues of both signs. Role axes inflate the covariance, consensus axes shift the mean, and the covariance-spectrum monitor sees only the former. The most concrete near-term task is to test the mean-field predictions in finite-population simulation, where fluctuations select which schema condenses. Two smaller questions follow: whether resource weighting generically widens spectral gaps, which would enlarge the monitoring window, and whether truthful status presentation can be made an equilibrium of a strategic-display extension \cite{lachmann2001}.

The formulation is deliberately minimal: one anti-coordination game, a Gaussian mean field, a loop that settles rather than perpetually transforms. It describes no real society, but serves as an idealization in the spirit of the harmonic oscillator---solvable, built around a single mechanism, and extended by perturbation. Here that mechanism is information-fuelled role differentiation and the cascade it drives. Whether the spectrum of a social gain matrix proves as useful across the social sciences as the oscillator's spectrum is across physics is the question we mean to raise.

\section{Microfoundations of the mean-field dynamics}\label{sm:micro}


The main-text role dynamics is the mean-field drift of an underlying encounter process. We record the reductions that produce it, since each is a modeling assumption with its own scope.

\paragraph{Encounter clock and the write rate.} Agent $i$ plays at Poisson times with rate $\nu$. At an encounter in context $m$ against partner $j$ it commits a signed decision $a\in\{-1,+1\}$ and inscribes a fixed increment $b_0\,a\,\g_m$ into $\w_i$, which otherwise relaxes:
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-micro-sde}
d\w_i = -\gamma\,\w_i(1+|\w_i|^2)\,dt + b_0\sum_m \g_m\,dN_{im}(t) + \sdyn\,d\bm B_i,
\end{equation}
with $N_{im}$ a counting process of rate $\nu f_m$ carrying signed jumps $a$. Taking the compensator, $\mathbb E[dN_{im}]=\nu f_m\langle a\rangle_m\,dt$, so the drift depends on $b_0$ and $\nu$ only through the product $\beta\equiv\nu b_0$: encounter frequency and per-encounter inscription are not separately identifiable at mean field. The same $\nu$ multiplies the coordination income (\cref{sm:fixedpoint}), so setting $\nu=1$ (time measured in mean encounter intervals) is a global units choice, not a per-equation one. $\beta/\gamma=\nu b_0/\gamma$ is then the number of times an identity is rewritten within its own decay horizon.

\paragraph{Signed decision and its noise average.} At the decision level the action is a hard sign of the integrated evidence, $a=\sign(\tfrac1T\sum_{t}s_t)$ on samples $s_t=\Delta+\sobs\xi_t$ of the true status difference $\Delta=(\w_i-\w_j)\cdot\g_m$. What enters the drift is its expectation over the observation noise,
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-micro-avg}
\langle a\rangle_m(\Delta)=\mathbb E_\xi\big[\sign(\bar s_T)\big]=\operatorname{erf}\!\Big(\frac{\sqrt T\,\Delta}{\sqrt2\,\sobs}\Big),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\langle a\rangle_m'(0)=2\pi'(0),\quad \pi'(0)=\frac{\sqrt{T/2\pi}}{\sobs}.
\end{equation}
The decision rule is a function of the observation $s$. The mean-field response is a function of the true status difference $\Delta$, the noise having been integrated out. It is this slope $\pi'(0)$, not the hard sign, that sets the gain: an un-averaged $\sign$ would give a singular (delta-function) susceptibility and an ill-posed threshold. Integrating $T$ samples sharpens the response as $\sqrt T$ (the read memory), whereas identity persistence $1/\gamma$ is the write memory. The two are distinct stages of the cycle.

\paragraph{Context normalization.} One context is drawn per encounter, with frequencies normalized so that $\sum_m f_m=1$. The drift $\beta\sum_m f_m\langle a\rangle_m\g_m$ is then an expectation over which context is played rather than a sum that grows with $M$, and the gain operator $A=\sum_m f_m\,\g_m\g_m^{\!\top}$ has $\operatorname{tr}A=\sum_m f_m u_m$, which stays $O(1)$ as contexts are added provided the bandwidth grows with the repertoire ($S_{\mathrm{bw}}\propto M$, i.e.\ fixed $\Theta$ of \cref{sm:regimes}) rather than being held fixed: adding contexts at fixed budget divides the same write rate more finely rather than driving identity harder. This read budget $\sum_m f_m=1$ is the counterpart on the read side of the bandwidth budget $\sum_m u_m\le S_{\mathrm{bw}}$ on the write side (\cref{sm:closedpool}). The dependence on how many contexts probe a $d$-dimensional space enters through the aspect ratio $M/d$ (\cref{sm:cascade}), not through an overall scale.

\paragraph{Pairing kernel and the well-mixed reduction.} Partners are drawn with probabilities $p_{ij}^{(m)}$ ($\sum_j p_{ij}^{(m)}=1$). Linearizing the partner-averaged response near the symmetric state,
\begin{equation}\label{SM-eq:sm-micro-lap}
\sum_j p_{ij}^{(m)}\,\langle a\rangle_m\big((\w_i-\w_j)\cdot\g_m\big)\approx 2\pi'(0)\,\g_m^{\!\top}\big((\mathbb{I}-\bm{P}^{(m)})\w\big)_i,
\end{equation}
so pairing enters the gain through the graph Laplacian $\bm{L}^{(m)}=\mathbb{I}-\bm{P}^{(m)}$ of the interaction network, and ignition is governed by the spectrum of $\sum_mf_m\,\g_m\g_m^{\!\top}\otimes \bm{L}^{(m)}$---a coupling of cultural space to social space. For \emph{well-mixed} pairing $p_{ij}=1/N$, $\bm{P}=\tfrac1N\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\!\top}$ projects onto the population mean, $\bm{L}$ acts as the identity off that mean, and the partner-average reduces to coupling to $\bar\w$ (a Curie--Weiss field). This recovers the gain $\G=2\pi'(0)\bm{P}^{\!\top}\bm{P}$ and the scalar pairing factor $(1-\rhopair)$ (the uniform diagonal reweighting of \smref{S2}). The results of this paper are the well-mixed (annealed) case. Structured $p_{ij}$---homophily, communities, degree heterogeneity---makes $\bm{L}^{(m)}$ nontrivial. It shifts thresholds and lets network structure, not only $\|\g_m\|$, select which role axis ignites first. Because homophily makes $p_{ij}$ depend on $\{\w\}$, the interaction network then co-evolves with the culture. We bracket this by keeping $p_{ij}=1/N$, which decouples network from culture and closes the loop analytically.


\paragraph{Shot noise.} At finite $\nu$ the compensated jump stream contributes shot noise of intensity $\nu b_0^2\sum_mf_m\g_m\g_m^{\!\top}=\beta b_0\,\bm{P}^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{f})\bm{P}$. This is proportional to the gain matrix $\G$ rather than isotropic, so it is aligned with the context directions and largest along the active axes. It is not a scalar renormalization of $\sdyn$: retaining it replaces the flat floor $\sdyn^2/2\gamma$ by a mode-dependent floor that grows with $\mu_k$. The poles of the covariance are unchanged, because the shot-noise covariance shares the eigenbasis of $(\gamma I-\beta\G)^{-1}$ and reweights only its numerator. The critical couplings $\beta_c^{(k)}$ and the relaxation-time spectroscopy are therefore identical in the two cases, and only the pre-onset amplitudes differ. The intensity is $O(\beta b_0)$ and vanishes in the dense-encounter limit ($\nu\to\infty$, $b_0\to0$ at fixed $\beta$), which leaves the isotropic $\sdyn^2\mathbb{I}$ that gives the flat noise floor used in \cref{sm:cascade}. We take this limit throughout.


\paragraph{Status microfoundation.} The signal $s_{im}=(\w_i-\w_j)\cdot\g_m+\sobs\xi$ is agnostic between a noisy read of a public status difference and exact self-knowledge minus a noisy read of the partner's self-stored status. The mean-field results are identical. We adopt the second reading---each agent stores its own status and presents a noisy version---so that no public registry is presupposed. This keeps the emergence claim non-circular (a public ledger is itself an institution, analyzed as operator technology in \smref{S4}) and matches the feedback term $\beta a_{im}$, which inscribes the agent's own act. Presentation is assumed truthful. The anti-coordination payoffs penalize inflated display (feigned high status recruits deference until it collides with genuine high status at $(-1,-1)$), so honesty is a plausible equilibrium of a strategic-display extension we do not develop here.

\section{Simulation details}\label{app:sim}

\paragraph{Analytics.} The coordination payoff $W=w_0(1-2P_e)^2$ follows from enumerating role-assignment errors in the Go/Defer game. The gain matrix $\G=2\pi'(0)\bm{P}^{\!\top}\bm{P}$ follows from mean-field linearization of \cref{eq:agent}. The self-consistent fixed point follows from a timescale-separated (agents $\to$ resources $\to$ schemas) analysis, in which agents equilibrate to the Boltzmann distribution of the gradient dynamics and resource-weighted covariances are evaluated by Wick's theorem in the Gaussian regime. The endogenous spectra follow from the resulting strength replicator. Full derivations are given in \crefrange{sm:gain}{sm:cascade} and \cite{SM}.

\paragraph{Simulation (Fig.~1).} $N=2500$ agents, $d=M=3$, Euler--Maruyama integration of the dense-encounter limit of \cref{eq:agent} (one random pairwise matching per step, all $M$ contexts played per encounter at $f_m=1$, threshold policy), $\gamma=1$, $\sobs=0.6$, $\sdyn=0.25$. Game directions are held fixed at a hierarchical strength profile ($|\g_m|$ = $1.55,\,1.1,\,0.72$; near-orthogonal), so the covariance eigenvalues $\lambda_k(\beta)$ resolve the cascade cleanly at $\beta_c^{(k)}=\gamma/[(1-\rhopair)\mu_k]$ with $\mu_k=2\pi'(0)u_k$, $u_k=|\g_k|^2$, and $\rhopair=0$ (factor $1$) for random pairing. The protocol is quasi-static: at each $\beta$ on a grid we equilibrate ($1.2\times10^3$ burn-in steps, warm-started from the previous $\beta$) and average the order parameter over a further $1.5\times10^3$ steps, so panels b,c are functions of $\beta$ rather than of time. The endogenous selection of the cascade ordering under dynamic schemas is treated in \cref{sm:closedpool}. The full dynamic-schema code is available in the repository given under Data availability.

\section{Platform control surface}\label{app:control}

The ignition and cascade factors are largely platform-owned, so an operator holds a pre-ignition control surface over which takeoff scenario is reachable (\cref{fig:monitor}). Three levers act cleanly on it. \emph{Ignition} is monotone in the channel factors $\rhopair$ and $\sobs$. Every agent must clear the cognitive floor, so raising channel noise or degrading matching shuts the engine down whatever the agents' quality. This lever acts entirely outside the models. Disassembly does not: removing individual agents leaves the resource-weighted inertia in place and the order stands, much as it does when spins are removed from a ferromagnet.
\emph{Cascade class} is steerable directly. The effective gain reweights the context Gram matrix by each context's play frequency times its pairing efficiency, $\bm{P}^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{f})\,\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\phi})\,\bm{P}$, so per-context rate limits (the frequencies $f_m$) and matching quality (the efficiencies $\phi_m$) are interchangeable levers. Optimizing that schedule splits a spiked spectrum into a geometric, extrapolable sequence (\smref{S2\,D}). In a representative repertoire it more than halves the log-spacing variance a monitor must resolve ($1.35\to0.58$) and costs roughly $40\%$ of the coordination efficiency. The same tradeoff recurs across the levers below: monitorability is obtained at the expense of coordination efficiency.
\emph{Sorting tempo} is set by the repertoire's seeding rather than by the ledger's memory: $r=\ract/g\propto1/\sseed$ (\cref{SM-eq:sm-nu}), so tiering task types by launch traffic, rather than opening them at equal weight, lowers $r$. Where the budget caps growth ($1<\Theta\le M$) this shifts the cascade off the avalanche side toward the extrapolable classes. In the thick band ($\Theta\le1$) the preference reverses at $r=M/L(\Theta)$, and a narrower seeding resolves the cascade instead (\cref{sm:regimes}). The operator defines the task taxonomy, so $\sseed$ is platform-owned in the same sense as $\mathbf{f}$ and $\bm{\phi}$ and acts on the same object, the spectrum of the effective gain (\smref{S4\,A}).
Two tensions bound the surface. Legibility trades against lock-in: the widely dispersed repertoire that yields the extrapolable classes is also the one in which the earliest and most strongly seeded contexts compound longest into entrenched incumbency, so the third lever improves legibility and deepens lock-in at once. A benign setting must also be \emph{held} rather than reached. The closed loop evolves toward condensation without intervention (\cref{sm:closedpool}), so the system returns to the avalanche-prone classes once the intervention is withdrawn. Which levers an operator holds at all depends on where identity is kept. A platform-maintained status ledger gives the operator $\beta$, $\gamma$, and the matching correlation $\rhopair$ together. If identity memory migrates into the agents' own stores, those levers pass to the collective, and that migration is itself an early and observable warning that control over the knobs is being lost (\smref{S4}).

\section{Schema directions and the vector form}\label{app:directions}

\Cref{eq:schema} evolves schema strengths on fixed axes. The general object is the vector reinforcement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schema-vec}
\dot\g_m=-\kappa\,\g_m+\alpha\,\Cr\,\g_m ,
\end{equation}
which carries the same two ingredients---covariance-driven and resource-weighted---but also lets a schema change which axis it prescribes. Writing $\g_m=\sqrt{u_m}\,\hat\g_m$ separates \cref{eq:schema-vec} exactly into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar-split}
\dot u_m = 2u_m\bigl(\alpha\lambda_m-\kappa\bigr),\qquad
\dot{\hat\g}_m = \alpha\bigl(\mathbb{I}-\hat\g_m\hat\g_m^{\!\top}\bigr)\Cr\,\hat\g_m ,
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_m=\hat\g_m^{\!\top}\Cr\hat\g_m$. The radial equation is \cref{eq:schema}. The angular equation is Oja's rule \cite{oja1982}: a power iteration on $\Cr$ constrained to the unit sphere.

\paragraph{Collapse of the angular sector.} The angular equation carries no $m$-dependence beyond its initial condition, since every schema is driven by the same matrix $\Cr$. Its stable fixed point is the leading eigenvector of $\Cr$ for all $m$, so $\hat\g_m\cdot\hat\g_n\to1$ and $\bm{P}^{\!\top}\bm{P}$ becomes rank one. By \cref{eq:cascade} a rank-one repertoire has a single critical coupling, so the cascade reduces to one bifurcation and the classification of \cref{fig:classes} does not apply. This is the collapse identified for the unweighted rotation $\dot\g_m=-\kappa\g_m+\alpha\C\g_m$ (\smref{S1\,A}). Resource weighting changes which matrix is iterated but not the fact that one matrix drives every schema.

The collapse is also the opposite of the transformation the theory is meant to describe. Rotation toward the dominant axis of $\Cr$ aligns every schema with the structure that already exists, which reproduces the leading role dimension rather than extending a schema to a context it was not built for.

\paragraph{Repelling mechanisms.} Sustaining distinct directions requires a term that separates schemas. Two are available in this model. Neither is contained in \crefrange{eq:agent}{eq:schema}.

\emph{(i) Imposed lateral inhibition.} Subtracting each schema's overlap with the others,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inhibition}
\dot{\hat\g}_m = \alpha\bigl(\mathbb{I}-\hat\g_m\hat\g_m^{\!\top}\bigr)\Cr\,\hat\g_m-\eta\sum_{n\neq m}(\hat\g_m\cdot\hat\g_n)\,\hat\g_n ,
\end{equation}
carries the repertoire onto distinct eigenvectors of $\Cr$ ordered by eigenvalue---the deflation of principal-subspace learning \cite{sanger1989}. It is effective but stipulated: the rate $\eta$ has no counterpart in the payoff structure, so the resulting repertoire is a property of the learning rule rather than of the game.

\emph{(ii) Marginal-return reinforcement.} The redundancy can instead be removed at its source. Coordination income sums over contexts, so two aligned schemas give $\Delta_{i1}=\Delta_{i2}$ and an agent collects $W$ twice for one binary role distinction, while the role information the pair supplies is $I$ rather than $2I$. Additive income therefore violates the Szilard bound of \cref{sec:model} whenever schemas are redundant. Charging each schema only its marginal contribution replaces $\lambda_m$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marginal}
\lambda_m^{\mathrm{marg}}=\hat\g_m^{\!\top}\Bigl(\mathbb{I}-\sum_{n\neq m}\hat\g_n\hat\g_n^{\!\top}\Bigr)\Cr\,\hat\g_m ,
\end{equation}
so a schema is reinforced only by variance no other schema already organizes. Rotation then moves toward uncovered variance, which is transposability in Sewell's sense, and the repelling term is derived from the payoff rather than added to the dynamics. The cost is that \cref{eq:resource} and the fitness of \cref{sm:closedpool} must both be re-derived, since income is no longer additive across contexts.

We adopt neither mechanism here. The questions this paper asks are ones the strength sector answers on its own.
